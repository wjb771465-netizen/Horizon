# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a general or geopolitical news item gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful information about public affairs, international relations, governance, or society.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Events that reshape geopolitics, national governance, major conflicts, landmark legislation, or crises with broad human consequences across regions or populations.
- **7-8: Important.** Clear developments with durable consequences for a country, alliance, large population, or international institution — elections with decisive outcomes, material policy shifts, significant diplomatic moves, or major disasters with verified impact.
- **5-6: Useful.** Credible reporting that helps readers understand an ongoing story or regional development but has limited breadth, novelty, or urgency.
- **3-4: Low value.** Incremental updates without new substance, speculative framing, recycled wire copy, or stories that lack verified actors, locations, or stakes.
- **0-2: Noise.** Rumors, sensational claims without sourcing, propaganda without independent corroboration, celebrity/sports filler, or content with no reliable public-affairs substance.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider magnitude and breadth of impact, source quality, timeliness, affected populations or institutions, and whether the item adds verified facts rather than speculation. Distinguish reported facts from allegations, anonymous claims, and unresolved rumors. Do not equate virality or emotional framing with importance.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
